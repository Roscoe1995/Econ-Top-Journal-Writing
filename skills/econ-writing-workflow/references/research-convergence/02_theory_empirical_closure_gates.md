# Theory And Empirical Closure Gates

Use only the sections relevant to the current paper and claim. Load the theory gate for theory, structural, or mixed papers; the measurement and identification gate for empirical claims; and the normative gate only when the manuscript makes welfare or policy recommendations.

Do not require every gate for local prose polishing or for a paper type that does not make the corresponding claim.

## Contents

1. Evidence-status gate
2. Theory and model closure
3. Economic-result qualification
4. Concept-to-measure bridge

## 1. Evidence-Status Gate

Classify each important claim as one of:

- `model_result`: derived from stated assumptions and equilibrium conditions;
- `numerical_result`: produced by calibration, simulation, or a quantitative exercise;
- `causal_estimate`: supported by an explicit identifying comparison and assumptions;
- `descriptive_fact`: documented without a causal interpretation;
- `suggestive_interpretation`: consistent with a mechanism but not uniquely identifying it;
- `planned_test`: proposed but not yet estimated or proved.

Keep the claim no stronger than its status. Do not convert correlation into causality, numerical illustration into a theorem, a necessary condition into a sufficient condition, local evidence into a general law, or a planned test into a completed finding.

Use claim verbs in context. `Proves`, `identifies`, `implies`, `supports`, and `suggests` are not a mechanical strength ladder: their validity depends on the model, design, assumptions, and comparison being stated.

## 2. Theory And Model Closure

For a baseline model, recover:

- agents and timing;
- choices and information;
- objectives and constraints;
- prices, interactions, or market-clearing conditions when applicable;
- state transitions when the model is dynamic;
- equilibrium or solution concept;
- the conditions that generate each stated result.

Do not use prose intuition to supply a mechanism absent from the formal setup.

For the central friction, answer:

1. Where does the friction enter?
2. Whose payoff, marginal return, information, or constraint does it change?
3. What is the relevant benchmark without it?
4. How does it change choice and the resulting equilibrium or path?

Classify assumptions as:

- **structural**: determines the economic mechanism or result;
- **technical**: ensures existence, uniqueness, tractability, or a valid derivation;
- **normalization**: simplifies units or notation without changing economic content.

Do not present a technical or normalization assumption as an economic contribution. State whether a key result survives relaxation of a structural assumption when that robustness matters.

Use model simplicity as a default, not an absolute rule. Apply a deletion test to mechanisms and assumptions, but retain complexity needed for structural identification, quantitative fit, general-equilibrium feedback, dynamic paths, or credible counterfactuals.

## 3. Economic-Result Qualification

A derivative sign, first-order condition, parameter appearing in a denominator, computable equilibrium, or mechanical monotonicity is not by itself an economic result.

For each main result, state:

- which behavior or equilibrium object changes;
- why the change occurs;
- how it differs from the relevant benchmark or existing explanation;
- what observable, comparative-static, dynamic, welfare, or institutional implication follows.

Treat four to six main results as a warning threshold for possible overexpansion, not as a universal cap.

## 4. Concept-To-Measure Bridge

For each central theoretical or conceptual object used empirically, record:

- the observable variable, proxy, constructed index, or explicit unobserved status;
- unit, timing, and level of observation;
- why the measure represents the concept;
- likely measurement error and its directional implications when known;
- plausible alternative measures;
- whether the measure may respond to the outcome or treatment itself.

Do not rename a convenient variable as the underlying concept without explaining the mapping. If the mapping is indirect, calibrate the mechanism and causal language accordingly.

## 5. Identification And Data-Capability Gate

Before treating a regression as evidence for the core claim, recover:

- why the treatment or explanatory variable varies;
- which variation is used for identification;
- why the comparison group or model counterfactual is credible;
- the strongest threat and the diagnostic or design response;
- which theoretical object the design actually identifies.

Separate what the data:

- estimate directly;
- support indirectly;
- cannot distinguish or identify in the current design.

Do not claim complete structural parameters, full welfare effects, unique mechanisms, or unobserved social value from a reduced-form or proxy-based design unless the required additional structure is present and defended.

## 6. Normative Policy Gate

Apply this gate only when the paper recommends intervention or makes a welfare claim.

Distinguish private and social benefits and costs, appropriable and non-appropriable returns, the relevant externality or distortion, information requirements, implementation and fiscal costs, enforcement risk, and behavioral or political distortions.

State policy claims conditionally: intervention may improve welfare when the measured or modeled social-private wedge is large enough to exceed implementation and distortion costs under the stated information and enforcement conditions.

Do not infer a subsidy, mandate, or regulatory recommendation from the mere presence of learning, spillovers, or heterogeneity.
